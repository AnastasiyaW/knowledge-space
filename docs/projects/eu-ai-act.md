# EU AI Act — Political agreement

## What it is

EU AI Act is Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 for companies that build, sell, integrate or use AI in the EU. It covers prohibited practices, high-risk systems, transparency and general-purpose AI models. Annex III high-risk rules apply from 2 December 2027; product-embedded Annex I rules apply from 2 August 2028. We classify the system and our role before treating a policy or model card as compliance.

## Development line

- **2023-12-10 — EU institutions reached a political agreement on the AI Act.** On 2023-12-10, EU institutions reached a political agreement on the EU AI Act. This moved negotiation toward a formal regulatory framework. The available links do not establish the final legal text, adoption sequence, or entry-into-force date. Public discussion and redistribution followed across the additional links.

## What changed

- 2023-12-10 — A provisional political agreement set the negotiated direction.
- 2024-06-13 — Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 was adopted.
- 2024-08-01 — The Act entered into force.
- 2025-02-02 — Prohibited-practice and AI-literacy provisions began to apply.
- 2025-08-02 — Governance and GPAI-model obligations began to apply.
- 2026-07-27 — The AI Omnibus changed the high-risk timetable.
- 2026-08-02 — Most provisions and Article 50 transparency obligations became applicable.
- 2027-12-02 — Annex III high-risk rules are scheduled to apply.
- 2028-08-02 — Annex I product-embedded high-risk rules are scheduled to apply.

## How to use this

From 2023-12-10, map affected AI systems and governance processes against the agreed EU AI Act direction. Treat final obligations and timelines as unconfirmed until the text is enacted.

1. Identify your role and EU exposure; use the official checker as a starting point, not legal advice.
  — <https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/eu-ai-act-compliance-checker>
2. Classify the system against prohibited practices, transparency duties and high-risk categories, retaining evidence and the applicable date.
  — <https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai>
3. If you provide a GPAI model, maintain authority and downstream documentation, copyright policy and training-content summary.
  — <https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/guidelines-obligations-general-purpose-ai-providers>
4. For interactive or generative systems, implement applicable Article 50 notices and machine-readable output marking before market placement or service use.
  — <https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/transparency-obligations-under-article-50-ai-act>

## Best practices

- Maintain a versioned inventory of systems, purpose, suppliers, EU exposure, role, classification evidence and review date.
  — <https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/guidelines-obligations-general-purpose-ai-providers>
- Use Commission guidance and the checker as operational aids; obtain tailored legal advice where the classification has material consequences.
  — <https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/eu-ai-act-compliance-checker>
- Use an approved code of practice or retain evidence that an alternative means is adequate.
  — <https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/transparency-obligations-under-article-50-ai-act>
